## Three design directions

These are alternatives, not ingredients to combine. The accompanying visual board shows illustrative compositions; product diagrams are not screenshots or live application data.

### 1. Product Studio — recommended

**Idea:** Let the quality of the interface demonstrate frontend ability before visitors read about it.

- Typography: precise sans serif, medium-weight display headings, restrained monospace captions. Proposed implementation: Geist Sans and Geist Mono through Next.js font loading.
- Color: ivory `#F5F4EE`, near-black `#18231F`, deep green `#245C48`, muted sage product stages.
- Composition: an asymmetric opening with a concise introduction and a large, carefully composed pharmacy workflow. Three substantial project chapters follow, with different visual treatments for each workflow.
- Hierarchy: product demonstration first, contribution second, engineering explanation third. Pharmacy leads, followed by Insights, then AI.
- Interaction: one keyboard-operable pharmacy batch-selection demonstration using clearly labelled sample data. Selecting a batch changes available quantity and a short explanation. Source-to-dashboard and record-to-answer diagrams explain the other projects. Native scrolling, visible links, short state transitions.
- Mobile: introduction then a compact demonstration; wide workflows become stacked steps. Case studies remain normal documents, not overlays.
- Mood: confident, contemporary, calm, approachable.
- Strength: strongest balance of frontend evidence and full-stack depth.
- Tradeoff: illustrative product scenes require careful fidelity and clear sample-data labels. They must not imply screenshots, live connections, or measured results.

### 2. Engineer’s Fieldnotes

**Idea:** Present the work as a personal collection of detailed engineering stories.

- Typography: expressive editorial serif headings with a neutral sans serif reading face; modest monospaced margin notes. Georgia is sufficient for the serif; no new package is required.
- Color: warm paper `#F0E7D8`, brown-black `#332B25`, burnt red `#99432E`.
- Composition: a broad typographic masthead followed by a narrow reading column and a small margin index. Full-width annotated figures interrupt the text at meaningful decisions.
- Hierarchy: the person and the problem lead; implementation evidence follows naturally in an article. Pharmacy, Insights, and AI are separate essays.
- Interaction: quiet anchor navigation, native disclosure for optional technical notes, and annotated figures. No simulated application in the opening.
- Mobile: the margin index becomes an inline contents list; annotations move directly beneath each figure.
- Mood: thoughtful, warm, literary, precise.
- Strength: most distinctive personal voice and best long-form reading experience.
- Tradeoff: frontend competence comes through typography, responsive layout, and detail; it is less immediately interactive than Product Studio.

### 3. Systems in Motion

**Idea:** Make the journey through a product visible: an action enters, a system changes, a person gets a useful result.

- Typography: compact bold sans serif display text, neutral readable body text, and monospaced diagram annotations.
- Color: midnight blue `#141E2C`, pale text `#EEF1E7`, soft citron `#D8EA91`, muted blue panels.
- Composition: a dark, wide opening organized around an architectural sequence. Large diagram stages lead into vertically scrolling project chapters; there is no dashboard shell or terminal styling.
- Hierarchy: behavior first, architecture second, implementation detail third. The pharmacy transaction and AI record-selection flows supply the strongest sequences.
- Interaction: an explicit “Trace the workflow” control advances through steps and highlights the corresponding explanation. No autoplay, scroll trapping, particle effects, or content hidden behind motion. Reduced-motion mode changes emphasis without movement.
- Mobile: every sequence becomes a vertical list with the explanation beside its step; the story remains readable without interaction.
- Mood: technical, focused, expressive.
- Strength: best at demonstrating architecture and purposeful motion together.
- Tradeoff: greatest implementation and accessibility effort; it can overshadow the human story unless the introduction stays direct.

### Pharmacy Management System

**From prescription to payment, in one connected workflow.**

I solely developed a pharmacy management system using Flutter Web, PHP Slim, and MySQL. It brings prescription intake, batch inventory, dispensing, billing, payments, and reporting into one product within Ayusmart SmartCare.

**The problem.** Dispensing medicine changes more than a shopping cart. Staff need to choose a particular stock batch, check how much is available, calculate a bill, record payment, and keep inventory in sync. Those steps need to agree even when a connected service is temporarily unavailable.

**What the interface does.** Staff can work from a prescription or a walk-in sale. A batch picker shows the batch number, expiry, source, and available quantity. The cart tracks the selected batch, supports quantity changes, and checks stock before submission. The dispenser interface also includes keyboard focus handling and a compact batch-selection layout for smaller screens.

**The important engineering decision.** The backend checks the selected stock row and locks it while processing the sale. It saves the bill, local stock changes, stock ledger, prescription status, and any inventory-sync event inside a database transaction. The remote inventory call happens after that transaction commits. This separates the completed local sale from the work needed to update the connected inventory service.

**Why that matters.** The bill refers to the exact batch that was selected, and the system has a stored record of the inventory update it needs to send. The portfolio can demonstrate this with a small illustrative sequence: choose a batch → review quantity → create bill → show inventory-sync state.

**Result.** The implementation covers the connected dispensing and billing workflow. The existing portfolio says the product is in user acceptance testing; retain that only as a previously supplied status until its current status is confirmed. There is no verified adoption count, measured time saving, or deployment outcome in this review.

**Scope of ownership.** Sole development is supported by the supplied résumé. The source review verifies the implementation mechanisms, not individual commit authorship.

### Ayusmart Insights

**Turning hospital records into data teams can report on.**

I built the backend and data pipelines behind Ayusmart Insights, connecting hospital records to operational dashboards built with Apache Superset.

**The problem.** Different hospital systems represent the same information differently. A dashboard needs consistent fields and dates before it can reliably summarize activity. Repeating those transformations manually makes each new report harder to maintain.

**How it works.** Configuration describes the source table, destination, field mappings, and update timestamp. A transformation layer renames fields, standardizes dates, trims text, and can assemble names. A synchronization runner reads records from the previous saved timestamp and sends the transformed records to the relevant API.

**The important engineering decision.** Mappings live outside the synchronization loop, so each dataset does not need a separate bespoke pipeline. The sender retries failed requests with increasing delays. Persistent failures are recorded in a separate log, while synchronization progress is recorded using timestamps.

**Why that matters.** The product work sits underneath the chart: making differently structured records usable by one analytics layer. An illustrated field-mapping example is a better portfolio artifact than unexplained bars and numbers. Label its records as synthetic.

**Result.** The inspected code supports configurable transformation, incremental synchronization, API delivery, retries, and failure logging. Superset integration is supported by the résumé and existing portfolio, not a live dashboard inspection. Do not promise continuous real-time updates or automatic creation of every dashboard from this evidence alone. Update frequency depends on the source and how synchronization is scheduled.

**Scope of ownership.** Describe backend and pipeline development separately from Apache Superset’s dashboard interface. Do not imply that the Superset visualization engine was built from scratch.

### Ayusmart AI Platform

**Helping clinicians review a patient’s history with the relevant records in view.**

I contributed across the frontend, backend, document processing, and AI orchestration of a healthcare AI platform. Its patient workflow starts with an explicitly selected patient and prepares relevant record context for a clinician-facing response.

**The problem.** Patient history can arrive as uploaded documents or structured hospital records. Before an AI response is useful, the system needs to select the correct patient, find the relevant encounters, and retain enough source context for the clinician to review the answer.

**How it works.** The current source contains two ingestion paths. Uploaded documents retain a staged document-processing and vector-indexing workflow. Structured FHIR records—the standard-shaped hospital data—are grouped by encounter and converted into SOAP notes: subjective information, objective findings, assessment, and plan. Those notes are stored in MySQL, archived in MinIO, and indexed in Qdrant for discovery.

**The important engineering decisions.** The encounter workflow compares a hash of the clinical source and the prompt version before generating a replacement note. If the source and prompt are unchanged, it can reuse the stored note. A publication-only operation can restore the archive and search index from MySQL without another model call. The chat path resolves requests such as “latest visit” directly against encounter records, uses patient-filtered search for topic discovery, and reloads the full notes from MySQL to assemble context.

**What the person sees.** The frontend requires patient selection before submitting a request. It has loading feedback, error messages, and source-citation presentation. The SOAP context includes source references and a notice when the answer uses only a subset of the known encounters.

**Result.** The inspected implementation supports patient-specific context preparation, structured encounter notes, and source-linked response preparation. The SOAP chat path is feature-flagged; its existence in code does not prove it is enabled in deployment. Clinical quality, authorization coverage, production status, and latency were not validated in this review. Do not present autonomous diagnosis, medical accuracy, compliance certification, or response-time improvements as established outcomes.

**Scope of ownership.** The résumé supports contributions to the healthcare AI/RAG platform and React/JSX/Vite workflows. The existing portfolio describes majority contribution; detailed personal attribution beyond that needs confirmation before expanding the claim.
